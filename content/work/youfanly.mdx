## 01. The problem

<Lead>Global creator platforms weren't built for Africa.</Lead>

African creators have fans who want to support them. But most subscription platforms aren't built around how Africans pay: local cards, naira pricing and payouts to local banks. That makes it harder for fans to subscribe and for creators to get paid.

The challenge: give African creators a simple way to share exclusive content, grow subscribers and get their money.

## 02. Discovery

<Lead>Two audiences, different needs, one platform.</Lead>

Fans and creators need each other, but they come to the platform for different reasons.

**Fans wanted to:**

- Discover creators and explore their content
- Subscribe to unlock paid posts
- Message creators
- Send tips

**Creators wanted to:**

- Publish, organise and schedule content
- Manage subscribers and talk to their fans
- Set up subscriptions and lock paid content
- Track earnings and wallet activity

Those needs shaped the biggest decision in the product: separate experiences for fans and creators.

## 03. Design decisions

### Two experiences, not one

Fans and creators use the same platform for different reasons. I split it into a content-led fan experience and a creator workspace for publishing, audience and earnings.

## 05. Impact

Designed in 2 months and now live, covering both sides of the platform: discovery, subscriptions, messaging, tips, publishing and earnings.

## 06. Reflection

<Lead>Designing for both sides of a marketplace.</Lead>

Fans and creators depend on each other, so neither side could feel like an afterthought. The hard part was keeping both connected without making either one complicated.
